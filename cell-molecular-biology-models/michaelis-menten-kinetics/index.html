Michaelis–Menten kinetics

Under standard assumptions, the enzyme reaction rate can be approximated by the Michaelis–Menten equation

\[v=\frac{V_{\max}[S]}{K_M+[S]}.\]

Here \(V_{\max}\) is the maximum reaction rate and \(K_M\) is the substrate concentration at which \(v=V_{\max}/2\).

Low and high substrate

When \([S]\ll K_M\), the rate is approximately proportional to substrate concentration. When \([S]\gg K_M\), the rate approaches \(V_{\max}\) and the enzyme becomes saturated.

Origin of the approximation

The formula follows from the enzyme–substrate reaction scheme using assumptions such as a quasi-steady concentration of the complex.

Key idea. Michaelis–Menten kinetics captures saturation: increasing substrate eventually produces little further increase in reaction rate.
