Turing patterns

A Turing pattern is a spatial pattern generated when diffusion destabilises an equilibrium that is stable in the corresponding non-spatial system.

Two-component system

\[\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=D_u\nabla^2u+f(u,v),\]\[\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=D_v\nabla^2v+g(u,v).\]

Without diffusion, the homogeneous equilibrium may be stable. With unequal diffusion rates, some spatial perturbations can grow.

Diffusion-driven instability

The mathematical test compares the reaction Jacobian with the diffusion coefficients and determines whether a spatial mode has positive growth rate.

Biological interpretation

Turing mechanisms have been studied as possible explanations for developmental and pigmentation patterns.

Key idea. In a Turing mechanism, diffusion does not simply smooth differences; together with reaction dynamics it can create stable spatial structure.
