SIR compartments and biological flows

An SIR model does more than store three populations. It describes how people move between the susceptible, infectious and recovered compartments. In this lesson, Python calculates these flows and updates the compartments over one short time step.

Biological scenario

A closed population contains 990 susceptible people, 10 infectious people and no recovered people. During the epidemic, susceptible people can become infectious and infectious people can recover.

We want to calculate both flows at the initial state and determine how the three compartments begin to change.

The SIR flow diagram

SSusceptible
→infection
IInfectious
→recovery
RRecovered
  • Infection moves a person from (S) to (I).
  • Recovery moves a person from (I) to (R).
  • No person enters or leaves the population.
  • The arrows show biological movement, not movement through physical space.

Mathematical-biology context

The infection flow depends on susceptible and infectious people being present. The recovery flow depends on the infectious population.

Infection flow[mathcal{F}_{mathrm{inf}}=eta rac{SI}{N}]
Recovery flow[mathcal{F}_{mathrm{rec}}=gamma I]

These flows determine the rates of change:

[ rac{dS}{dt}=-mathcal{F}_{mathrm{inf}}]
[ rac{dI}{dt}=mathcal{F}_{mathrm{inf}}-mathcal{F}_{mathrm{rec}}]
[ rac{dR}{dt}=mathcal{F}_{mathrm{rec}}]

The same infection flow is subtracted from (S) and added to (I). The same recovery flow is subtracted from (I) and added to (R). Therefore, people are transferred rather than created or destroyed.

Programming objective

We will calculate the infection and recovery flows, use them to obtain the changes in (S), (I) and (R), and update the population through one time step of length (Delta t).

Build the code step by step

Step 1: Calculate the two biological flows

infection_flow = beta * S * I / N
recovery_flow = gamma * I

These statements translate the two mathematical flow formulas directly into Python. Descriptive variable names make the biological meaning visible in the program.

Step 2: Calculate the compartment rates of change

dS_dt = -infection_flow
dI_dt = infection_flow - recovery_flow
dR_dt = recovery_flow

The minus sign means that a compartment loses people. A positive contribution means that people enter the compartment.

Step 3: Convert rates into changes over a short time

change_S = dS_dt * dt
change_I = dI_dt * dt
change_R = dR_dt * dt

A rate is multiplied by the time-step length dt. This gives the approximate change during that interval.

Step 4: Update the compartments

S_new = S + change_S
I_new = I + change_I
R_new = R + change_R

The updated value equals the current value plus its approximate change. This is one forward Euler update; the method will be studied carefully in Lesson 6.

Run the complete program

Change the values if you wish, then select Run code. Use a small value such as dt = 0.1 because this update approximates continuous change over a short interval.

Biological interpretation

At the initial state, the infection flow is 2.97 people per day and the recovery flow is 1 person per day. Over 0.1 day, the model therefore transfers approximately 0.297 people from (S) to (I) and 0.100 people from (I) to (R).

The infectious compartment increases by approximately (0.297-0.100=0.197). The total remains 1,000 because each loss from one compartment is an equal gain to another.

Decimal compartment values are expected in a deterministic model. They represent continuous approximations to population-level behaviour, not literal fractions of an individual event.

Important model and programming conditions

  • (S), (I) and (R) must remain non-negative.
  • (N=S+I+R) must be conserved in this closed-population model.
  • (etageq0), (gammageq0) and (Delta t>0).
  • The time step should be sufficiently small for the Euler update to be accurate.
  • abs(N_new - N) < 1e-10 allows for very small floating-point rounding differences.
