Expected infectious population and its variance

Moment solutions become biologically useful when expected burden and variability are interpreted separately and together.

Two complementary summaries

\[\mu_I(t)=\mathbb E[I(t)],\qquad \sigma_I^2(t)=\operatorname{Var}(I(t)).\]

The mean describes the ensemble centre at each time. Variance describes path-to-path spread around that mean. Neither is one realised epidemic.

Standard deviation and coefficient of variation

\[\sigma_I(t)=\sqrt{\sigma_I^2(t)},\qquad \mathrm{CV}(t)=\frac{\sigma_I(t)}{\mu_I(t)}.\]

Standard deviation has units of people. CV is dimensionless and measures variation relative to the mean, but becomes unstable when the mean is close to zero.

Mean ± SD is not automatically a probability interval

For a normal distribution, approximately 68% lies within one standard deviation of the mean. Epidemic distributions may be skewed, bounded or contain extinction mass, so this interpretation must not be assumed from moments alone.

Biological meaning

A high expected infectious population suggests high average burden. A high variance means scenarios with the same parameters can differ substantially. The timing of maximum uncertainty can be different from the timing of maximum expected burden.
