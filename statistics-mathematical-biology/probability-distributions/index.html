Probability distributions

A probability distribution describes the possible values of a random variable and how probability is assigned to them.

Discrete example

A binomial variable counts successes in \(n\) independent Bernoulli trials:

\[P(X=k)=\binom nk p^k(1-p)^{n-k}.\]

Its mean and variance are \(np\) and \(np(1-p)\).

Continuous example

A normal variable is described by mean \(\mu\) and variance \(\sigma^2\):

\[X\sim N(\mu,\sigma^2).\]

Biological choice

Counts, proportions, waiting times and continuous measurements have different mathematical properties, so the distribution should reflect how the data arise.

Key idea. A probability distribution is a mathematical model for uncertainty in a measured or generated quantity.
