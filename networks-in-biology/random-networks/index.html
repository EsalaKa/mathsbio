Random networks

A random-network model treats some aspect of network structure as probabilistic.

Erdős–Rényi model

In the \(G(n,p)\) model, each pair of \(n\) nodes is connected independently with probability \(p\).

The degree of a node follows

\[K\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n-1,p),\]

so its expected degree is

\[E[K]=(n-1)p.\]

Why use random networks?

They provide mathematical reference models and allow uncertainty in connection structure to be studied. Other random-network models can preserve a specified degree distribution or include clustering and communities.

Key idea. Random networks describe connection patterns probabilistically rather than specifying every edge in advance.
