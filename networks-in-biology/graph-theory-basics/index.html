Graph theory basics

A graph represents a system as a set of nodes connected by edges. In biology, nodes may represent individuals, species, genes or cells, while edges represent interactions.

A graph is written

\[G=(V,E),\]

where \(V\) is the set of vertices and \(E\) the set of edges.

Adjacency matrix

For \(n\) nodes, the adjacency matrix \(A\) has entries

\[A_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if nodes }i\text{ and }j\text{ are connected},\\0,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}\]

Graphs may be directed or undirected and edges may also carry weights.

Key idea. Graph theory provides a mathematical language for representing who or what interacts with whom.
