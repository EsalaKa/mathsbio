Ecological networks

Ecological networks represent interactions among species or ecological groups.

Food webs

In a food web, directed edges represent feeding relationships between consumers and resources. Network structure can reveal trophic levels, pathways of indirect effects and highly connected species.

Other interactions

Networks can also represent competition, mutualism, host–parasite relationships and plant–pollinator interactions.

From structure to dynamics

An interaction network specifies who affects whom. Population equations, such as generalised Lotka–Volterra systems, can then attach strengths and dynamical consequences to those edges.

Key idea. Ecological networks organise many pairwise biological interactions into a system-level representation.
