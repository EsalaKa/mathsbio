Biological networks

Many biological systems are naturally represented as networks because their behaviour depends on patterns of interaction rather than on isolated components.

Examples

Contact networks connect individuals who can transmit infection. Gene-regulatory networks connect regulators and target genes. Food webs connect consumers and resources. Protein-interaction networks connect molecules that physically or functionally interact.

Structure and dynamics

Two systems containing the same number of components can behave differently if their connection patterns differ. Network models therefore separate the identities or states of components from the topology linking them.

Key idea. Network structure can influence biological dynamics independently of the number of components in the system.
