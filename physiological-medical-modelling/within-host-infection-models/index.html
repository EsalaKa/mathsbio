Within-host infection models

Within-host models describe infection processes inside an individual rather than transmission between individuals in a population.

Target-cell model

A standard viral model uses target cells \(T\), infected cells \(I\) and free virus \(V\):

\[\frac{dT}{dt}=-\beta TV,\]\[\frac{dI}{dt}=\beta TV-\delta I,\]\[\frac{dV}{dt}=pI-cV.\]

Here \(\beta\) controls infection of target cells, \(\delta\) infected-cell loss, \(p\) viral production and \(c\) viral clearance.

Extensions

Immune responses, drug treatment, latent infection and multiple target-cell types can be incorporated when required by the biological question.

Scale distinction

Within-host viral load can influence infectiousness, but within-host and population-level epidemic models operate at different biological scales.

Key idea. Within-host models study pathogen replication and host response inside one organism, complementing population-level epidemiological models.
